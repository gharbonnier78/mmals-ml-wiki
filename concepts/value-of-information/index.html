Decision under uncertainty

Value of information

The improvement in expected decision value enabled by information, net of the cost or delay required to obtain it.

general decision conceptadded 2026-09-01

Intuition

Information is not valuable because it is precise. It is valuable when it can change later choices in a way that improves expected outcomes.

\mathrm{VoI}(e\mid b)=V_H^*(b;\,e\ \mathrm{available})-V_H^*(b;\,e\ \mathrm{unavailable})-c(e)

Concrete example

Suppose both a 60% and a 90% fault probability still lead to the same low-cost safe action. A perfect diagnostic may greatly reduce uncertainty but have almost no decision value. Near a boundary where the result determines repair versus continued operation, the same diagnostic can be valuable.

Mathematical descent

Compute the best expected finite-horizon value when the evidence action is allowed. Compute the best value without it. Subtract the evidence-action cost. The difference measures what the option to learn adds to the decision problem.

In plain language

“How much better can I act because I learned this, after paying for the learning?”

Misconception

Wrong: entropy reduction, confidence increase and value of information are interchangeable. Correction: a belief may become much sharper without changing any consequential action.

Understanding gate

Explain why “more certainty” can have zero value of information, then construct a nearby case where the same evidence becomes decision-changing.

Project relevance

This distinction is central to GO-ED-POMDP-style evidence acquisition, but the project-specific framework is not treated here as an externally established theory.
