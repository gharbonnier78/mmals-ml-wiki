Collective forecasting

Meta-prediction

A forecast about what other forecasters are expected to report, used to characterize shared information or latent expertise.

source-derived conceptadded 2026-09-26stable /go/ link

Discover

Give your own probability and also estimate the average probability you expect other people to give.

Engineer

Own forecasts p_i and meta-predictions m_i can be used jointly by aggregation methods designed to account for shared information without requiring long individual track records.

Research

Published meta-prediction methods differ in assumptions and algorithms; project-specific transforms require prospective evaluation and explicit provenance.

Formal anchor

m_i\approx\mathbb E_i[\bar p_{-i}]

Own forecast: p_i=P(Y=1\mid I_i); meta-prediction: m_i estimates the peer-average forecast expected by participant i.

Concrete example

An engineer assigns 70% to an event but expects colleagues to average only 50%. That difference may indicate private information, but it may also reflect projection or a mistaken model of colleagues.

Misconception

Wrong: mᵢ is the true shared prior. Correction: it is another noisy human judgment and can itself be biased.

Understanding gate

Explain why own forecasts alone may not reveal which evidence is common to all forecasters, and why meta-predictions still do not reveal shared information perfectly.

Authority

See Palley & Soll (2019), Martinie et al. (2020) and Peker & Wilkening (2025) in the forecasting source synthesis.
