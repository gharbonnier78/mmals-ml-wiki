Semantics

Mycorrhizal exchange

The governed mutualistic relationship through which hosts and the fungal medium exchange useful resources or information.

Modeling hypothesisreviewed 2026-09-03stable /go/ link

In plain language

The exchange is mutualistic when the relationship remains beneficial and sustainable for the participating artificial components over the declared horizon. “Mutualistic” is a deliberate MMALS design criterion, not a claim that nature universally rewards every participant.

Engineering view

A practical proxy removes host i from the mixture and measures the change in loss. Positive contribution gain means the host was useful for that batch or context. Costs can include entropy, latency, energy, memory, or communication. Stronger operational evidence should also expose reciprocity, persistent asymmetric cost, non-regression, recoverability, and behavior in the presence of low-contribution or exploitative hosts.

Research view

Mutualism is not equivalent to positive average gain in one run, nor does every participant need to gain on every interaction. Strong evidence requires a declared horizon, longitudinal reciprocal benefit, resource accounting, causal interventions, robustness to exploitation, and reproducibility across seeds and contexts.

Modeling hypothesis
Current evidence

Contribution-gain terms and ablations have been implemented; robust ecosystem-level mutualism remains an open research target. Separate evolutionary-game evidence shows that conditional partner-specific responses can support cooperation under bounded assumptions, but that result does not establish MMALS mutualism.

Boundary

Biology includes cooperation, competition, predation and parasitism. The mycorrhizal metaphor therefore does not establish a universal natural rule of mutual benefit: in MMALS, mutualism is a normative and operational target whose proxies remain model-dependent.
