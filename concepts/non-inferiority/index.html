Non-inferiority

A non-inferiority study asks whether a candidate is not worse than a reference by more than an acceptable, predeclared margin.

Evaluation conceptreviewed 2026-08-24stable /go/ link

In plain language

Sometimes a smaller, cheaper or faster system does not need to beat the reference. It may be useful if the loss in quality is small enough. Non-inferiority formalizes “small enough” before looking at the result.

Engineering view

Define a degradation estimand such as candidate FNMR minus reference FNMR at the same false-match operating point. Freeze a maximum acceptable degradation delta. The candidate is supported only if the uncertainty bound stays below delta under the preregistered decision rule.

Research view

The margin is part of the scientific claim, not a tuning knob. It should be justified from decision consequences, prior evidence or domain tolerance before outcome inspection. Failure to demonstrate non-inferiority is not logically identical to demonstrating inferiority.

Formal anchor

Delta = loss(candidate) - loss(reference); support non-inferiority only if UCB(Delta) < delta
Claim discipline
Misconception

“The candidate failed non-inferiority, therefore it is proven worse.” Incorrect: the evidence may simply be too uncertain or the margin too demanding.

Understanding gate

Explain the roles of the reference, estimand, margin, one-sided uncertainty bound and operating point.
