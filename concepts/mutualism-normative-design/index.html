Mutualism as a normative design principle

Reciprocal long-horizon benefit can be a requirement we deliberately impose on an artificial learning ecosystem; it is not a universal law of living systems.

open hypothesisadded 2026-09-03

In plain language

Nature contains cooperation, exploitation, competition, predation and parasitism. If MMALS chooses “Mutualistic” as a design value, that choice comes from what we want the artificial system to preserve—not from claiming that nature is always mutually beneficial.

Engineering view

Mutualistic behavior should be operationalized through longitudinal contribution, reciprocal benefit, resource/energy cost, non-regression, recoverability and resistance to exploitative participants. A temporary local loss can be acceptable if the interaction remains sustainable over the declared horizon.

Research view

The scientific question is whether a mutualistic regime can emerge from conditional interaction and continual adaptation without being trivially hard-coded into the reward. Compare partner-agnostic, partner-conditioned and explicitly mutualism-regularized policies, including exploitative-host controls.

Formal anchor

Mutualistic = explicit design constraint + measurable reciprocal benefit + bounded cost
open hypothesis
Current evidence

MMALS already measures contribution proxies, while separate evolutionary-game evidence shows that cooperation can emerge from opponent-specific responses under bounded assumptions. Neither result establishes MMALS-level mutualism.

Boundary

“M = Mutualistic” is normative project semantics. It must not be justified as a descriptive law of biology.

Relation to mycorrhizal exchange

The existing mycorrhizal-exchange concept describes the governed interaction and its measurable proxies. This page adds the governance meaning: mutualism is a target property to falsify or support longitudinally, not an assumption embedded in the biological metaphor.

Misconception

Wrong: every participant must gain on every interaction. Correction: the relevant horizon, allowed temporary cost, reciprocity rule and non-exploitation condition must be declared explicitly.

Understanding gate

Describe a case where one host incurs a short-term cost but the interaction can still be called mutualistic, and state what evidence would distinguish that from persistent exploitation.
