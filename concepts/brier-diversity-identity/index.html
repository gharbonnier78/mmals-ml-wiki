Brier diversity identity

Exact squared-error decomposition relating the Brier loss of an arithmetic mean to average individual loss and forecast diversity.

mathematical identityadded 2026-09-26stable /go/ link

Discover

Averaging can cancel part of individual squared errors; the identity says exactly how much disagreement is subtracted.

Engineer

The mean forecast's Brier loss equals mean individual Brier loss minus the average squared deviation of forecasts around their mean.

Research

This is an algebraic identity, not an optimality theorem for pooling independent evidence.

Mathematical descent

BS(\bar p,y)=\frac1n\sum_i BS(p_i,y)-\frac1n\sum_i(p_i-\bar p)^2

Expand both squared terms and use Σ(pᵢ−p̄)=0. The cross term vanishes, leaving the identity.

Concrete example

For forecasts 0.4 and 0.8 with y=1, the mean is 0.6. Mean individual loss is (0.36+0.04)/2=0.20; forecast diversity is ((−0.2)²+0.2²)/2=0.04; mean-forecast loss is 0.16=0.20−0.04.

Misconception

Wrong: because diversity improves the average in this identity, a simple mean always aggregates information optimally. Correction: the identity describes squared-error geometry; it does not reconstruct the joint likelihood of independent clues.

Understanding gate

Derive the identity for two forecasts and explain why it concerns error cancellation rather than full Bayesian evidence pooling.
