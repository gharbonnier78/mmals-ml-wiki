Brier score

A squared-error score for probabilistic forecasts of binary events: the probability is compared directly with the realized 0/1 outcome.

foundational conceptadded 2026-09-25stable /go/ link

Discover

If you say 80% and the event happens, your error is small; if you say 20% and it happens, your error is large. The Brier score squares that probability error.

Engineer

For binary outcomes encoded as 0 or 1, lower Brier loss is better. It keeps probability information that a 50%-threshold accuracy metric would discard.

Research

The Brier score is a proper scoring rule. Average Brier differences can compare forecast methods on paired events, but dependence between events and finite-sample uncertainty must be respected.

Mathematical descent

A binary outcome y is represented by 0 or 1. A forecast p lies in [0,1]. Their error is p-y; squaring removes the sign and penalizes larger probability errors:

BS(p,y)=(p-y)^2

Concrete example

If y=1: BS(0.8,1)=0.04 while BS(0.2,1)=0.64.

In plain language

The score asks: how far was your announced probability from what eventually happened?

Misconception

Wrong: a lower Brier score on one event proves a method is better. Correction: one resolved event is one paired observation; method comparison requires a suitable set of events and uncertainty analysis.

Understanding gate

Compute BS(0.7,0) and explain why p=0.51 and p=0.99 would both be “correct” under threshold accuracy if y=1 but receive very different probabilistic scores.

Authority

See Brier (1950) and Gneiting & Raftery (2007) in the source synthesis.
