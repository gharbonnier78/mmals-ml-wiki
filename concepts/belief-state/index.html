Belief state

A probability distribution representing current uncertainty about a hidden state, updated as new observations and actions arrive.

foundational conceptexpanded 2026-09-01

Intuition

The true state may be hidden. Instead of pretending to know one state exactly, maintain probabilities over the possibilities still compatible with the evidence.

Concrete example

A sensor reports “warning”. If that observation is common in both healthy and faulty regimes, one warning should not force a hard regime label. The posterior belief shifts according to the prior belief and the observation model.

Mathematical descent

The history contains past observations and actions. Bayes filtering compresses that history into a posterior over current hidden state. Under a correctly specified POMDP, the belief itself is a Markov information state for planning: predict through the transition model, incorporate the next observation, then plan from the updated belief.

In plain language

The belief says “given everything I have seen and done, how plausible is each current hidden state?”.

Why more certainty may not matter

A belief can move from 0.60 to 0.90 on one hypothesis without changing the preferred action if both values lie on the same side of the relevant decision boundary. This is why belief precision and decision value must be kept distinct.

Misconception

Wrong: a belief should be frozen to prevent forgetting. Correction: beliefs should remain revisable by evidence. What may need protection is validated competence, constraints, provenance or model components.

Understanding gate

Explain why the whole observation history can sometimes be discarded after computing the belief, and name the assumptions under which that compression can fail.

MMALS relevance

A future MMALS core could maintain beliefs over hosts, regimes or latent system states instead of committing prematurely to one inferred context. Route weights are not automatically beliefs unless the semantics and update law establish that equivalence.
