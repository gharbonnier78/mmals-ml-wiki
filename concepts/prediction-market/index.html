Information aggregation

Prediction market

A mechanism in which participants express probabilistic beliefs through trades or score-linked updates and a market state aggregates those beliefs.

foundational conceptadded 2026-09-25stable /go/ link

Discover

If the current collective probability is 55% and you believe the event is closer to 75%, an appropriate market mechanism gives you a reason to move the price upward. People with opposite information push the other way.

Engineer

The market is not a neural network: there are normally no learned hidden-layer weights or backpropagation. The state changes because participants act on differences between their own beliefs and the displayed aggregate. Market-maker rules, liquidity, visibility and incentives are experimental configuration.

Research

Market prices should be treated as forecasts produced by a mechanism. Corporate evidence exists, but biases, thinness, public-information overweighting and social structure can affect aggregation.

Formal anchor

p^M_t=M( ext{orders}_{le t}; heta_M)

Concrete example

A binary contract resolves to 1 when a frozen engineering criterion is met and 0 otherwise. A market closes at 0.68. The number is evaluated later as a probability forecast; it is not itself a GO decision.

What fails if structure is ignored?

If mechanism parameters, initial price, participant visibility or AI/news assistance are not frozen, two apparently identical “markets” may not be comparable or reproducible.

Misconception

Wrong: the market price is the truth probability. Correction: it is an aggregate conditional on participants, information, incentives and mechanism.

Understanding gate

Explain how a market can update a probability without training a neural network, and name two sources of market bias.

Authority

See Hanson for market scoring rules and Cowgill/Zitzewitz for corporate evidence in the source synthesis.
