Verification operating point

A verification system is judged at a threshold, and that threshold jointly determines false matches and false non-matches.

Evaluation conceptreviewed 2026-08-24stable /go/ link

In plain language

A face-verification system does not simply output “good” or “bad”. It produces a score, and a threshold decides whether two samples are treated as a match. Move the threshold one way and you reject more genuine users; move it the other way and you accept more impostors.

Engineering view

FMR/FAR describes false accepts among impostor attempts. FNMR/FRR describes false rejects among genuine attempts. TAR/TMR is the corresponding genuine acceptance rate, so under matching semantics FNMR = 1 - TAR. Decision-relevant evaluation therefore freezes an operating point such as FMR=10^-4 and reports FNMR there.

Research view

Threshold-aggregated summaries such as AUC or EER can diagnose ranking quality, but they do not replace a preregistered operating-point criterion. Two systems can have similar AUC and materially different FNMR at the low-FMR region that matters operationally.

Formal anchor

operating point = threshold τ together with (FMR(τ), FNMR(τ))
Decision semantics
Key relation

When benchmark semantics align, TAR(τ)=1-FNMR(τ).

Misconception

“Best EER means best production operating point.” Not necessarily: EER is one descriptive balance point, while production may require a much lower FMR.

Understanding gate

Explain why lowering FMR generally changes FNMR, and why a fixed-FMR comparison is more decision-relevant than one global scalar.
