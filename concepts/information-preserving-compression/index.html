Information and representation

Information-preserving compression

Reducing representation complexity while preserving the information required for a specified prediction, reconstruction or decision.

foundational bridgeexpanded 2026-09-02

Preserve what?

Compression quality is target-relative. Preserving classification accuracy can still destroy transition structure, uncertainty, causal variables or future decision value.

Same engineering pattern, different mathematical objects

Projecting a learned representation to fewer dimensions, quantizing model weights, selecting a smaller active context and compressing an action-observation history into a decision state all reduce something. They are not the same mathematical operation and should not share an evaluation criterion by analogy alone. The useful common pattern is: reduce representation or execution cost, then test the downstream properties that the mission says must survive.

For a representation projection, those properties might include discrimination, geometry or calibration. For an agent state, they may include policy value, uncertainty and the ability to update after later observations. For an edge implementation, they may additionally include latency, memory footprint and energy. A smaller object is useful only if the relevant loss remains acceptable.

Signal-theory bridge

Sampling and reconstruction motivate the broader question of how structural assumptions determine the minimum information needed to recover what matters. MMALS must test its own sufficient-information conditions rather than import Nyquist as an analogy-made-law.

Edge inference as one resource regime

A 2026 AGX Orin measurement campaign provides an engineering example rather than a universal law. On that board and workload, the lowest instantaneous-power mode was not the lowest-energy configuration per generated token, while quantization and speculative decoding changed the energy/work trade-off substantially. The same campaign also measured a higher output-token energy coefficient with a long loaded context. These observations support treating active context, model configuration and decoding strategy as resource-sensitive implementation choices, not treating energy as the universal objective.

Source note — Becirovski AGX Orin energy campaign.

MMALS relevance

The “minimal sufficient” objective should penalize complexity only after defining what information must survive: prior competence, dynamics, regime boundaries, calibrated uncertainty and decision consequences. Resource cost belongs beside those requirements, with its importance determined by the mission and operating conditions.

Toy

Compress a latent state to progressively smaller dimensions and measure not only current accuracy but transition prediction, regime discrimination and decision regret. In a resource-constrained variant, record memory, latency or energy as additional outcomes rather than silently replacing the decision criterion with one of them.

Misconception

Wrong: because two techniques reduce memory or energy, they implement the same kind of compression. Correction: weight quantization, latent projection and history/state compression act on different objects; the defensible bridge is the task-relative preservation test, not mathematical identity.
