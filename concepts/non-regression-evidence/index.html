Continual learning and assurance

Evidence of non-regression ≠ proof of no regression

Finite replay, anchors or sentinels can provide bounded evidence that old competence remains intact; they do not generally prove that a changed model is identical everywhere outside the tested set.

assurance boundaryadded 2026-09-01

Intuition

If a model changes, testing a hundred old cases can show that those cases still work. Without stronger structural assumptions, it cannot certify every untested point in a large state space.

L_{old}(H^{t+1})\le L_{old}(H^t)+\varepsilon

Concrete example

After adapting a host, replay a curated set of old-regime sentinels and compare outputs, latent behavior and decision metrics. Passing gives evidence for preserved competence on that coverage set. It is not a universal theorem about all old-regime inputs.

Stronger routes

Stronger assurance can come from architectural isolation/freezing, verified invariants, certified bounds, or mathematically constrained updates. Each protects a declared property; none should be described as global non-regression unless its assumptions really support that statement.

In plain language

“I checked that old capabilities still work where I looked” is different from “I proved nothing changed anywhere important”.

Misconception

Wrong: a finite sentinel set proves no catastrophic forgetting. Correction: it supplies evidence whose strength depends on coverage, representativeness and the structure of the function/model class.

Understanding gate

State what additional assumptions would be needed to turn sampled non-regression evidence into a mathematically global guarantee.

MMALS relevance
