Accountable human canonicalization

A claim cannot become canonical merely because automated verification succeeded. At least one accountable human must be able to explain the result, its evidence, limitations, provenance, and significance.

Harness-derived governance rulereviewed 2026-08-21stable /go/ link

In plain language

A green check is not the same thing as understanding. A machine can verify a proof, rerun a notebook, validate a schema, or approve a test result. Before the result becomes durable knowledge, a responsible human should still be able to explain what was shown, why the evidence is credible, what the result does not prove, where it came from, and why it matters.

Engineering view

Use automated gates to establish bounded states such as verified, reproduced, or all_checks_passed. Promotion to an organizational reference, standard pattern, release rationale, or canonical design requires an explicit accountable-human understanding gate. This applies to LLM- and agent-assisted architecture, test strategy, IaC, qualification evidence, risk assessment, and decision support.

Research view

The rule separates artifact validity from knowledge assimilation. It is motivated by Terence Tao's 2026 analysis of AI-era mathematics, where problem solving expands from generation to verification, exposition, community digestion, and canonicalization. The scientific-research-harness generalizes that observation beyond mathematics: automation can increase artifact abundance faster than expert verification and assimilation capacity.

Canonical rule

automated acceptance ≠ canonical knowledge

A claim cannot reach canonical status solely because an automated verifier accepted it. At least one accountable human must be capable of explaining the result, its evidence, limitations, provenance and significance.

Closure test

A result is ready for canonicalization only when an accountable human can reconstruct and explain:

  • the question or decision;
  • the assumptions and scope;
  • the result;
  • the evidence and verification path;
  • the provenance and authoritative sources;
  • the important limitations and uncertainties;
  • material failed hypotheses, counterexamples, ablations, or reversals when they changed understanding;
  • the significance of the result and what it does not establish.

Why the chronicle matters

Highly polished AI output can hide where the real intellectual difficulty occurred. Diderot therefore treats failed paths and explanatory friction as potentially useful knowledge when they materially changed the research trajectory. The goal is not to archive private reasoning traces, but to preserve externally inspectable scientific events that changed what was believed, tested, rejected, or done next.

From harness to Diderot

The scientific-research-harness governs how a result becomes canonicalization-ready. Diderot is a downstream canonicalization layer: it turns sufficiently understood, reviewed, connected, and teachable results into durable reference knowledge. Passing the harness gate does not automatically force Diderot to accept a concept.

Application to AI-augmented engineering governance

The same principle applies to LLM- and agent-assisted engineering production. Automated validators and reviewer agents can increase confidence, but they cannot remove accountability. Before an AI-assisted architecture, test strategy, release recommendation, infrastructure definition, risk analysis, or qualification result becomes an organizational reference, at least one accountable human must be able to explain its authoritative inputs, evidence, limits, provenance, operational implications, and significance for the governed decision.

Source and derivation

Primary motivation
Source

Terence Tao, Mathematics in the Age of AI, arXiv:2608.16753v1, 2026.

Source-supported observations

The paper distinguishes proof generation from verification, exposition, publication/acceptance, digestion, and canonicalization; warns that AI may create proof abundance and bottlenecks downstream; discusses Goodhart effects; and argues that a formally verified proof that no human can properly explain remains incomplete.

Generalization

The extension from mathematics to scientific-research harnesses and engineering governance is a design inference of this project, not a claim made directly by Tao.
