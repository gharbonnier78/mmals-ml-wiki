Proper scoring rule

A rule for evaluating probabilistic forecasts whose expected score is optimized by reporting the forecaster's true predictive distribution under the scoring assumptions.

foundational conceptadded 2026-09-25stable /go/ link

Discover

A probability should be rewarded for being both confident when justified and cautious when uncertainty is real. A proper scoring rule is designed so that bluffing with a fake probability is not advantageous in expectation.

Engineer

Use a proper score when the output is a probability rather than a hard label. Brier loss and logarithmic loss are standard examples, with different sensitivity to extreme probabilities.

Research

Strict propriety is a property of the expected score under a predictive distribution. It does not imply that an observed finite sample is calibrated, unbiased or causally informative.

Formal anchor

mathbb E_{Ysim Q}[S(Q,Y)] ge mathbb E_{Ysim Q}[S(P,Y)]

For a loss convention, reverse the inequality: reporting the true distribution minimizes expected loss.

Concrete example

For a binary event that occurs, Brier loss is 0.04 at p=0.8 and 0.64 at p=0.2. The confident wrong forecast is penalized more heavily.

Misconception

Wrong: “proper” means the forecasting system is calibrated. Correction: propriety concerns the evaluation/incentive rule; calibration is an empirical relationship between probabilities and frequencies.

Understanding gate
