Proper scoring rule

A rule for evaluating probabilistic forecasts whose expected score is optimized by reporting the predictive distribution actually believed, under the scoring assumptions.

foundational conceptrevised 2026-09-26stable /go/ link

Discover

A good probability score should not make bluffing with fake certainty advantageous in expectation.

Engineer

Use a proper score when the output is a probability. Brier and logarithmic scores are examples; their behavior differs especially near 0 and 1.

Research

Strict propriety is an expectation-level property of the score. It does not imply that a finite set of forecasts is calibrated, unbiased or causally valid.

Expected-loss example

mathbb E[BS]=0.7(1-p)^2+0.3p^2

If the forecaster's true belief is q=0.7, the expected normalized binary Brier loss is minimized at p=0.7: the minimum is 0.21, whereas reporting p=1 gives expected loss 0.30.

Misconception

Wrong: a score is proper merely because it punishes confident errors. Correction: absolute error also punishes large misses; propriety is about expected optimization at the true belief.

Understanding gate

Differentiate a proper scoring rule from calibration and explain why one realized outcome cannot demonstrate propriety empirically.
