Observability and reconstruction

How much partial information is sufficient to recover the state or the relevant dynamics of a system?

foundational bridgeadded 2026-08-24

Signal intuition

Sampling theory shows that strong structural assumptions can make a continuous signal reconstructible from discrete observations. The lesson is not that every system obeys Nyquist, but that structure determines how much information is sufficient.

Dynamical intuition

Observability asks whether hidden state can be inferred from available measurements. Delay-embedding results such as Takens' theorem show that, under assumptions, a sequence of observations of one variable can preserve the geometry of an attractor.

Y(t)=[y(t),y(t-\tau),\ldots,y(t-(m-1)\tau)]

MMALS relevance

The candidate question is not “how many features can we store?” but “what is the smallest observed or latent state that preserves the competence, transition structure, uncertainty and decisions that matter?” Compression that destroys those properties is not success.
