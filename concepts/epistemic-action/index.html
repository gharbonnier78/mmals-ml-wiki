Information-gathering decision

Epistemic action

An action chosen partly because of what it can reveal, not only because of its immediate physical or operational payoff.

general decision conceptadded 2026-09-01

Intuition

A test, inspection, probe, benchmark or staged deployment can be worthwhile because its outcome changes what should be done next.

Concrete example

A hidden machine fault can be repaired immediately or inspected first. Inspection costs time, but a positive result justifies repair while a negative result avoids unnecessary work.

b_t\xrightarrow{\;a_t=\mathrm{probe},\;o_{t+1}\;}b_{t+1}

Mathematical descent

The action changes the distribution of the next observation. The observation updates the belief. The new belief changes later action values. Therefore part of the action's value comes through the information channel rather than through a direct reward.

In plain language

You sometimes act in order to know what to do, not merely to finish the task immediately.

Misconception

Wrong: an information-gathering action is useful whenever it reduces uncertainty. Correction: lower uncertainty has decision value only when the information can improve later choices enough to justify its cost.

Understanding gate

Give an example where a test makes the belief much sharper but is still not worth performing because both possible outcomes lead to the same later action.
