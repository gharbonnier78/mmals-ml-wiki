Sequential decision making

Policy

A rule for choosing what to do from the information available now. A policy is not one fixed future action sequence: it can choose differently after different future observations.

foundational conceptadded 2026-09-01

Intuition

An action answers “what do I do now?”. A policy answers “how will I keep choosing as the situation changes?”. Under partial observability the input to that rule can be the current belief state.

a_t=\pi(b_t)

Concrete example

A machine-health belief may justify continue now. After a diagnostic observation, the same policy may choose repair if the posterior risk rises or continue if it falls. The later action is not fixed before the observation arrives.

Mathematical descent

Start with a belief bt that summarizes current uncertainty. A deterministic policy maps that belief to one admissible action. A stochastic policy instead specifies a distribution over actions. Re-applying the policy after every belief update produces contingent behavior.

In plain language

The policy is the decision rule that survives across time; the current action is only one output of that rule.

Misconception

Wrong: planning means writing a fixed list a₀,a₁,a₂,… in advance. Correction: under uncertainty, good planning normally permits later actions to depend on observations not yet seen.

Understanding gate

You should be able to explain why two runs can start with the same first action but later diverge under the same policy after receiving different observations.

Authority
