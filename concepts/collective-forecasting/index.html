Collective forecasting

Combining multiple probabilistic judgments to estimate an uncertain future event while keeping the aggregation rule explicit.

foundational conceptadded 2026-09-25stable /go/ link

Discover

Several people may each know a different piece of the situation. Collective forecasting asks them for probabilities and combines those probabilities instead of forcing an early yes/no consensus.

Engineer

The aggregation rule is part of the system. A simple mean, weighted pool, prediction market and human–machine ensemble are different mechanisms and must be evaluated against the same resolved events.

Research

The useful scientific question is not whether “the crowd is wise” in general, but whether a defined aggregation mechanism adds information beyond explicit baselines under a known information structure.

Formal anchor

p_{agg}=A(p_1,ldots,p_n)

Why it matters

Distributed engineering knowledge can be partly tacit or role-local. A probabilistic aggregate makes disagreement and uncertainty measurable without turning them into a governance vote.

Concrete example

Integration, test and platform specialists privately assign 0.35, 0.65 and 0.80 to the same objectively resolvable event. The arithmetic mean is 0.60; a market may produce a different aggregate after interaction. Both remain forecasts to score against the later outcome.

Misconception

Wrong: more people automatically make the aggregate correct. Correction: correlated errors, common misinformation, social pressure and poor question design can make a large group confidently wrong.

Understanding gate

Explain why a prediction market and an arithmetic mean can use the same individual forecasters yet constitute different aggregation mechanisms.

Evidence and boundary

Corporate and experimental literature supports studying collective aggregation, but no source establishes universal superiority for engineering qualification. See the source synthesis.
