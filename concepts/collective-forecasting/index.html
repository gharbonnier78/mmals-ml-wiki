Collective forecasting

Combining multiple probabilistic judgments to estimate an uncertain event while making the aggregation rule and comparator explicit.

source-derived conceptrevised 2026-09-26stable /go/ link

Discover

Several people may hold different pieces of evidence. Instead of voting yes/no, ask each for a probability and combine the answers under a declared rule.

Engineer

The mean, median, meta-belief recalibration, prediction market and human–machine ensemble are different aggregators. They should be compared on the same events and against what the organization already knew, not against an empty baseline.

Research

The key question is conditional: which aggregation mechanism adds information under which information structure? Shared information, correlated errors and actor/observer composition can change the answer.

Formal anchor

p_{agg}=A(p_1,ldots,p_n)

Concrete example

Four engineers privately report probabilities 0.35, 0.50, 0.65 and 0.80. Their mean is 0.575, but a different fixed aggregator may use additional information such as meta-predictions. The aggregate must later be scored against the same resolved event and institutional references.

Misconception

Wrong: adding more forecasters automatically makes the crowd correct. Correction: common misinformation and correlated errors can remain after aggregation.

Understanding gate

Explain why a strong collective-forecasting study needs both an explicit aggregation rule and an explicit reference forecast.
