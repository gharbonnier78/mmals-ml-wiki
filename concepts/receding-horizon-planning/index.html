Receding-horizon planning

Plan a bounded distance into the future, execute only the next admissible step, observe what actually happens, then update and plan again.

general control patternadded 2026-09-01

Intuition

Long decision trees grow exponentially. Receding-horizon planning avoids pretending that one initial plan will remain correct after new evidence arrives.

\mathrm{plan}_H(b_t)\to a_t\to o_{t+1}\to b_{t+1}\to \mathrm{plan}_H(b_{t+1})

Concrete example

At each engineering checkpoint, simulate the next three steps. Execute only the first decision. Once test evidence arrives, discard branches inconsistent with the observation and solve the next short-horizon problem again.

Mathematical descent

At time t, optimize over a horizon H. Apply the first action of the selected policy. Replace the predictive belief with the posterior belief after observation. Shift the horizon forward and solve again. This is a control strategy, not a guarantee of global optimality.

In plain language

Think ahead enough to avoid being myopic, but keep the right to revise the plan when reality answers back.

Misconception

Wrong: replanning means the previous plan was useless. Correction: the previous plan selected the action and information that produced the new state from which replanning continues.

Understanding gate

Explain why a bounded-horizon replanner can implement contingent behavior without explicitly storing an exponentially large full policy tree.

Boundary

This page describes a general pattern. It does not claim that MMALS currently implements model-predictive control or that a fixed horizon is always adequate.
