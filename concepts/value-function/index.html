Value and action value

Value asks how good a future policy is from the current information state; action value asks how good it is to take one action now and then continue according to a policy.

foundational conceptexpanded 2026-09-02

Intuition

A next-step score answers a local question. A value function deliberately includes later consequences. This is why an action that costs something now can still be best if it enables better choices later.

V_H^\pi(b_t)=\mathbb E\!\left[\sum_{k=0}^{H-1}\gamma^k r_{t+k}\mid b_t,\pi\right]
Q_H^\pi(b_t,a_t)=\mathbb E\!\left[r_t+\gamma V_{H-1}^\pi(b_{t+1})\mid b_t,a_t\right]

Concrete example

A diagnostic probe costs 1 unit immediately. If its result allows the next step to avoid a 20-unit failure, its multi-step value can be positive even though its immediate reward is negative.

Mathematical descent

Choose a finite horizon H. Add immediate reward/cost at the current step. Discount later terms by γ if the model uses discounting. Take the expectation over uncertain transitions and observations induced by the policy. Q fixes the first action; V evaluates the continuation rule.

One mission objective can contain several engineering costs

For an engineered agent, the immediate reward/cost need not represent one physical quantity. It can account for task success, failure consequence, latency, energy, memory pressure, monetary cost, evidence acquisition and human-review burden. A weighted scalar objective is one possible engineering choice, but the weights are not universal constants: they should reflect the declared mission and operating conditions. In a battery-limited edge regime, energy may receive much more importance; in another regime, latency, safety or answer quality may dominate.

The objective itself may also change when the mission changes. Some requirements should be modeled as hard constraints rather than traded away through a small weight—for example, a safety or authorization condition that must hold regardless of an energy saving. This is why “energy-aware” is a useful special case, not the general optimization problem.

Regime information must be available to the decision

If cost priorities depend on an operating regime that is directly observed, the policy can condition on that context. If the regime is hidden or uncertain, a planner should not use the unknowable true label as if it were available; it must condition on the information state, such as a belief over possible regimes, or on another justified observable representation.

In plain language

Q is “if I do this first, then behave according to the policy, how good is the resulting future?” V is “from here, how good is the policy overall?”. The word “good” is meaningless until the mission objective and non-negotiable constraints are declared.

Misconception

Wrong: the action with the best immediate reward must have the best Q-value, or the cheapest-energy action must be the best engineering action. Correction: delayed consequences, competing costs and hard constraints can reverse the ranking.

Understanding gate

Given two actions where one pays now and the other reveals information, explain how the information-gathering action can have lower immediate reward but higher multi-step Q-value. Then name one change of mission or operating regime that would legitimately change the cost trade-off without changing the physical system.
