Predictive models

World model

A family of internal predictive models that represent enough relevant state and transition structure to reason about how an environment can evolve, often under candidate actions.

general conceptMMALS relation conditionalextended 2026-08-30

No single canonical definition

The term is used differently across model-based reinforcement learning, robotics, representation learning and recent generative AI. Diderot therefore treats this page as a bounded working concept, not a normative definition.

Minimal working structure

z_t=E(o_{\le t}),\qquad p(z_{t+1}\mid z_t,a_t)

A useful world model usually needs some combination of state estimation, transition dynamics, uncertainty and the ability to evaluate meaningful future consequences. Not every implementation exposes all these objects explicitly.

What is not enough

An encoder alone, a current classifier, continual-learning memory, visually plausible generation, inferred context or adaptive routing do not by themselves establish a world model.

MMALS boundary

Present MMALS should not be relabeled as a world model. The relation becomes substantive only if future work validates predictive transition dynamics, a sufficient current state or belief, and—where planning/control is claimed—action-conditioned consequences.

GO-ED-POMDP bridge

POMDP belief states provide a disciplined representation of uncertainty about hidden current state. GO-ED adds the decision question: which goal matters, what evidence supports the belief, what evidence is missing, and whether further observation is worth its cost.

Toy before claim

Use a hidden-regime POMDP toy before any architectural claim. Compare full history, current observation, latent state and belief state; then add an action-conditioned transition model and test prediction, calibration and decision regret.
