## Source / interpretation separation

The biological sources support a bounded critique of naïve biomimicry: living systems should not be assumed to be globally optimized, intrinsically unlimited in resilience, universally mutualistic, or independent of evolutionary history. They also motivate distinguishing biological responses that operate at different time scales.

The following are **Diderot/MMALS interpretations**, not claims established by those biological sources:

1. **Viability vs optimization** — test whether declared competence/resource/safety bounds should constrain adaptation before scalar optimization.
2. **Path dependence** — test whether selected history-derived information adds value once a strong current-state/sufficient-state baseline is available.
3. **Multi-scale adaptation** — make reaction → within-model adaptation → structural fork/new-regime nomination an explicit escalation hypothesis.
4. **Mutualism as normative design** — treat `M = Mutualistic` as a chosen long-horizon reciprocal-benefit property, not a descriptive law of nature.

## Decisions recorded

- Keep all four new concepts at **open hypothesis** status.
- Do not use biological plausibility as evidence of MMALS superiority or mechanistic validity.
- Preserve the existing narrow validated MMALS core; the new concepts are linked research frontiers only.
- Strengthen the mycorrhizal-exchange boundary: cooperation, competition, predation and parasitism coexist in biology; MMALS mutualism must therefore be operationally defined and measured.
- Require a declared horizon, resource accounting, non-regression evidence, reciprocity and exploitative-host controls before calling a result mutualistic.
- Retain history only when its incremental predictive, decision, causal, audit or competence-preservation value is demonstrated; do not equate path dependence with full-history storage.
- No new non-trivial mathematical notation was introduced in this change; the notation registry is therefore intentionally unchanged.
