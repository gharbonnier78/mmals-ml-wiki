# Source note

## Central contribution

Asenbaum and Overstreet challenge the interpretation of the phase measured in the Quantum Galileo Interferometer. They argue that the observable phase can be understood as a magnetic recoil phase and that the final observable does not depend on the uniform gravitational field in the way claimed by the original preprint.

## What the source actually provides

The comment presents an alternative theoretical calculation and physical interpretation of the same interferometric observable. Its central claim is that the measured phase is analogous to recoil phases in light-pulse interferometers, depends on inertial mass, and does not directly establish sensitivity to gravitational mass.

For the Diderot case study, the important source-derived fact is not that this critique is necessarily correct, but that a technically explicit competing explanation was proposed for the same observed signal.

## Limitations and uncertainty

This is a technical arXiv comment rather than a final peer-reviewed adjudication. The original authors contest its conclusion in a separate reply. It therefore must not be presented as having invalidated the experiment or as establishing a consensus interpretation.

## Consequence for current work

Reviewer inference: this source makes the Science Advances case unusually useful for teaching identifiability. Agreement between an observable and a prediction does not by itself exclude another model that can account for the same observable. The next evidential question becomes which measurement or intervention would discriminate between the explanations.

That reasoning transfers directly to MMALS and engineering qualification: a successful test of an expected signal can support a claim while leaving the assumed mechanism underdetermined.
