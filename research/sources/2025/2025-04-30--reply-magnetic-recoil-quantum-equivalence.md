# Source note

## Central contribution

Dobkowski et al. respond to the magnetic-recoil critique by arguing that, contrary to the comment, the Quantum Galileo Interferometer remains sensitive to a uniform gravitational field both with and without the levitation condition.

## What the source actually provides

The reply develops the authors' theoretical counterargument to the claim that the observed phase is independent of a uniform gravitational field. It therefore records the second side of an explicit scientific disagreement over the interpretation of the measured phase.

For Diderot, the source is valuable because it prevents a one-sided narrative: the existence of an alternative explanation is evidence of an identifiability question, not automatic evidence that the alternative explanation has prevailed.

## Limitations and uncertainty

This is an author reply on arXiv, not an independent final adjudication of the dispute. Its existence narrows and clarifies the disagreement but does not justify presenting either interpretation as uniquely established solely from these two preprints.

The later peer-reviewed Science Advances paper adopts a more bounded title and conclusion centered on the observed quantum phase of free fall and consistency with the equivalence principle.

## Consequence for current work

Reviewer inference: the comment/reply pair provides a compact example of why evidence assessment needs at least two questions: **Did we observe what the model predicted?** and **Does that observation discriminate this model from credible alternatives?** Those questions should remain separate in MMALS, hypothesis-testing, and Test Authority reasoning.

## Follow-up

Keep this reply linked to the technical comment and final peer-reviewed article. If the case study is expanded later, formulate a candidate discriminating experiment rather than turning the unresolved interpretive dispute into a rhetorical conclusion.
