# Primary-source pack

This note records the primary publications used to check the September 2026 *Pour la Science* article on collective intelligence. It deliberately separates **what each source establishes** from **the MMALS/harness interpretation**.

## Claim/source matrix

| Source | Primary result relevant here | Important boundary | Diderot/MMALS use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Woolley et al. (2010), *Science* | In two studies with 699 participants in groups of 2-5, performance across different group tasks loaded on a general collective-intelligence factor `c`; the paper reported associations with social sensitivity and equality of conversational turn-taking, and only weak association with average/maximum individual intelligence. | The correlates are not by themselves a complete causal mechanism and were challenged by later replication work. | Treat `c` as evidence that reusable group-level performance structure can exist, not as a recipe saying turn-taking alone creates intelligence. |
| Bates & Gupta (2017), *Intelligence* | Three replication studies (312 participants) attributed much more group-performance variance to individual IQ and did not support the reported gender-balance or turn-taking effects. | This is counter-evidence to the stronger causal interpretation of Woolley et al.; it does not show that collaboration process never matters. | Keep the causal decomposition explicitly unsettled. |
| Riedl et al. (2021), *PNAS* | Meta-analytic/pooled analysis across 22 studies, 5,279 individuals and 1,356 groups again found a general collective-intelligence factor and showed that collaboration-process measures can predict collective performance beyond individual-member characteristics. | Human small-group data; no direct evidence for LLM agents. | Separate contributor capability from collaboration protocol as two empirical dimensions. |
| Hong & Page (2004), *PNAS* | In a formal framework of functionally diverse problem solvers, a diverse selected team can outperform a team of the individually best solvers when high performers become similar in their problem-solving representations/heuristics. | Conditional theorem/model result; not a universal empirical claim that any diversity is beneficial. | Prefer measured functional/error diversity over nominal agent count or demographic analogy. |
| Bernstein, Shore & Lazer (2018), *PNAS* | Randomized problem-solving experiments found a trade-off: constant interaction improved average performance through social learning but reduced exploration; intermittent interaction preserved more of both benefits. | Specific human task and group setting. It does not validate an LLM Scientist/Reviewer cadence. | Motivates, but does not prove, an intermittent interaction arm in an MMALS experiment. |
| Kurvers et al. (2016), *PNAS* | Pooling independent medical judgments improved diagnostic performance; benefits depended on group composition and were strongest when additional contributors were sufficiently competent rather than merely numerous. | Medical image/diagnostic task; group aggregation is not free-form deliberation. | Distinguish diversity of errors from heterogeneity so large that low-quality contributors degrade the aggregate. |
| Prelec, Seung & McCoy (2017), *Nature* | Proposed the "surprisingly popular" rule: select answers that are more popular than respondents predict, recovering specialized minority knowledge under stated assumptions better than simple popularity/confidence baselines in their studies. | Requires eliciting both an answer and a prediction about others; assumptions and task form matter. | Candidate aggregation mechanism, not a default reviewer rule. |
| Zöller et al. (2025), *PNAS* | Across 2,133 clinical vignettes, hybrid human-LLM collectives outperformed the compared individual and homogeneous collective baselines by exploiting complementary strengths/failure patterns. | Primarily independent diagnoses plus algorithmic aggregation; not evidence that conversational human-AI debate is superior. | Strong motivation to measure **unique-error recovery** and error correlation in hybrid/heterogeneous ensembles. |
| Danús et al. (2026), *PNAS* | In ~130,000 political-science articles, acknowledgment-network connections were associated with publication productivity/impact beyond coauthorship-network ties after matching/model controls. | Observational association, not a causal test of decision quality or collective intelligence. | Adjacent evidence for informal cognitive infrastructure only; do not use as proof of the MMALS collaboration mechanism. |

## Mathematical identity: diversity prediction theorem

For numerical predictions `s_i`, truth `theta`, and mean prediction `c = (1/N) sum_i s_i`:

```text
(c - theta)^2
  = (1/N) sum_i (s_i - theta)^2
  - (1/N) sum_i (s_i - c)^2
```

This is an algebraic identity under arithmetic-mean aggregation and squared error. It shows that the error of the mean can be decomposed into mean individual squared error minus prediction diversity.

**Do not infer the causal statement "increase diversity and accuracy must improve."** In an intervention, diversity and individual error can change together. For MMALS the observable of interest is therefore not diversity alone, but the joint profile:

- individual competence/error;
- pairwise/shared error structure;
- prediction or hypothesis diversity;
- aggregate loss.

## Evidence boundary for MMALS/harness

The sources support a research question, not a design verdict.

**Supported by primary literature:** independence, diversity/complementarity, aggregation design and interaction structure can materially affect collective performance in human groups or human-AI diagnostic ensembles.

**Not established:** that the current MMALS Scientist/Reviewer architecture, any LLM debate protocol, or an "independent -> synchronize -> independent -> challenge -> aggregate" cycle improves scientific reasoning.

That transfer must be tested experimentally.
