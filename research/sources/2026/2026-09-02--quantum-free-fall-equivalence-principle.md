# Source note

## Central contribution

The paper reports a cold-atom interferometer in which one matter-wave packet remains static in the laboratory frame while another is in free fall. The measured relative phase follows the predicted free-fall phase, and the authors conclude that, in the low-energy regime tested, the observation is consistent with applying the equivalence principle in the quantum domain.

For Diderot/MMALS, the paper is especially useful as a case study in the distinction between observing an expected signal and uniquely identifying the physical explanation of that signal.

## What the source actually provides

Source-derived evidence: a direct interferometric measurement of the relative phase between static and freely falling cold-atom wave packets, with agreement with the phase behavior predicted for the experiment. The published claim is deliberately bounded to consistency with the equivalence principle in the tested low-energy regime.

The paper does **not** demonstrate that gravity itself is quantized, does not provide a general theory of quantum gravity, and should not be cited as proving that quantum mechanics and general relativity have been unified.

The publication history is also epistemically informative. The 2025 preprint was titled *Observation of the quantum equivalence principle for matter-waves* (arXiv:2502.14535), whereas the peer-reviewed article uses the more calibrated formulation *Observation of the quantum phase of free fall and the consistency with the equivalence principle*.

## Limitations and uncertainty

The experiment is confined to a cold-atom, low-energy regime. Generalization to larger masses, stronger gravitational regimes, or quantum gravity is unsupported by this result alone.

A technical interpretive dispute preceded the final publication. Asenbaum and Overstreet argued that the measured phase can be interpreted as a magnetic recoil phase and does not directly depend on the uniform gravitational field (arXiv:2504.15409). Dobkowski et al. replied that the Quantum Galileo Interferometer remains sensitive to a uniform gravitational field even without the levitation condition (arXiv:2504.21626). The existence of this dispute means that measurement agreement and mechanism identification must remain separate claims.

## Consequence for current work

Source-derived lesson: the experiment supports a bounded empirical statement about an observed phase and its consistency with a theoretical prediction.

Reviewer inference for Diderot/MMALS: use this as a concrete example of the chain **claim → predicted observable → measurement → competing explanation → discriminating evidence → residual uncertainty**. A measurement can strongly support a claim without uniquely identifying the mechanism that generated it. Evidence strength and explanatory identifiability are different dimensions.

This maps directly to the existing *From hypothesis to evidence* pathway and to the broader engineering rule that a PASS against an expected observable does not automatically establish that the assumed causal model is the only compatible explanation.
