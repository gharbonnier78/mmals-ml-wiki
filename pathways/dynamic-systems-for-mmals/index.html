Guided interdisciplinary pathway

From observation to adaptive world models

A no-jump route from observations and state inference through POMDP beliefs, transition dynamics, stochastic/chaotic regimes, local geometry, MMALS adaptation and the conditional world-model frontier.

research bridgeextended 2026-08-30
Boundary

This pathway connects concepts because they may supply useful tools. It does not claim that MMALS is already a world model, that latent space is automatically a physical manifold, or that analogies from signal theory and physics transfer without hypotheses and tests.

1

Encoder → State

Separate a representation mechanism from the stronger semantic claim that its output is a state relevant to dynamics.

2

Latent state → Sufficient state

Ask what hidden information the representation carries and what it must preserve for the target prediction or decision.

3

Markov property → State estimator

Test whether the current state summarizes enough relevant history, and how such a state can be inferred from partial observations.

4

MDP → POMDP → Belief state

Move from observable state to hidden state and explicit uncertainty over what is currently true.

5

Bayesian filtering

Maintain belief through recursive prediction and evidence update instead of reclassifying each observation independently.

6

Transition model + Observation model

Separate how the world changes from how that hidden change becomes observable.

7

Observability, sampling and delay reconstruction

Ask how much partial information is sufficient to reconstruct the state or dynamics that matter.

8

Information-preserving compression

Compress only relative to explicit preservation targets: competence, transition structure, uncertainty and decision value.

9

Stochastic dynamics vs deterministic chaos

Separate random forcing from sensitive deterministic dynamics and from plain model ignorance.

10

Stochastic-chaotic system + Lyapunov exponent

Understand why exact trajectories may diverge while structural or probabilistic prediction can remain useful.

11

Attractor → basin → bistability

Represent persistent dynamical organization without confusing it with ordinary clustering.

12

Langevin/SDE → committor

Model noisy transitions and predict the probability of consequential regime change rather than only one exact path.

13

Recurrence + local dimension + local geometry

Ask whether local neighborhoods preserve similar dynamical fate and whether complexity varies by regime.

14

Dynamical regime + dynamic compatibility

Use multi-evidence compatibility rather than one latent-space distance to decide whether reuse remains plausible.

15

Minimal sufficient dynamic inference

Prefer reuse, then adaptation, then fork, and only then candidate regime birth when evidence requires additional structure.

16

Action-conditioned prediction → rollout → planning

Move from passive prediction toward evaluating alternative future consequences.

17

Counterfactual + controllability

Distinguish simulated alternatives, causal claims and what the agent can actually influence.

18

Toy model / experiment

Before scale, isolate mechanism and failure modes. Toy evidence remains mechanism evidence, not general superiority.

19

World model

Only now ask whether the resulting state, dynamics, uncertainty and action-conditioned predictions justify the term for MMALS.

Unifying question

What is the smallest auditable adaptive model that preserves the state, uncertainty and transition structure required to make reliable decisions about an evolving complex system?

Scientific use

Every bridge should eventually produce a source, a toy, an experiment, an ablation, a counterexample or a stop condition. Elegant concepts that do not improve explanation, prediction, calibration, control, cost or falsifiability remain pedagogical rather than entering the MMALS core.

What would falsify or weaken this direction?

The program should be narrowed or rejected if adaptive forks grow without measurable benefit; if a fixed mixture-of-experts or standard continual-learning baseline matches competence, retention and calibration at lower total complexity; if candidate regime births are mostly artifacts of representation distance, sensor shift or noise; if compact state loses decision-relevant transition information; or if local geometry, committor-style transition risk and action-conditioned dynamics add no predictive or decision value over simpler baselines. Failure of one bridge does not falsify every other bridge, but repeated absence of incremental value should prevent the overall synthesis from entering the MMALS core.
